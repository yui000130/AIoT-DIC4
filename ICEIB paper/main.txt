ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Retail participation in the Taiwan stock market reached a record 14.827 million
accounts by October 2026, yet long-term equity wealth creation remains
extremely concentrated. Century-scale empirical evidence shows that just 46 of
29,754 U.S. stocks generated half of the USD 91 trillion in net shareholder
wealth, while in Taiwan a single firm (TSMC) accounts for over 40% of total
market capitalization and small-to-mid-cap electronics stocks averaged 171%
returns during the 2025–2026 AI supply-chain cycle. Identifying such winners
ex ante is notoriously difficult: 79% of active U.S. large-cap funds
underperformed in 2025 with zero five-year top-quartile persistence,
survivorship bias inflates backtest returns by 30–100%, and large language
models (LLMs) suffer from look-ahead leakage and numerical hallucinations
(>10–20% error rates on multi-variable financial calculations).

To address these challenges, we propose Multibagger Scout, a point-in-time
(PiT) AI research agent for long-horizon equity screening in Taiwan. Using
FinMind financial statements, monthly revenues, and price histories, the
system defines a composite target label—ranking in the top 5% of five-year
dividend-adjusted total return with revenue CAGR >= 10%—while explicitly
retaining delisted stocks to eliminate survivorship bias. We compare LightGBM,
logistic regression, and single-factor baselines under a strict Program-of-
Thoughts (PoT) separation: deterministic tools execute all numerical
computations, whereas the LLM is restricted to tool orchestration, SHAP
feature summarization, and structured pro/con synthesis, followed by a
programmatic verifier. Literature benchmarks show PoT eliminates 88% of
arithmetic errors and LLM news analysis carries predictive value. We design a
2013–2020 feature window with three walk-forward test rounds evaluated via
Precision@K, PR-AUC, and report numerical accuracy. Preliminary data pipeline
feasibility has been verified; full backtest validation against the 5% random
base rate remains ongoing. This system is a research tool and does not
constitute investment advice.

Keywords: Point-in-Time Equity Screening; AI Agent; Program of Thoughts;
Survivorship Bias; LightGBM; Taiwan Stock Market

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 Background and Motivation
Long-term equity returns exhibit severe positive skewness. According to the
2026 century-long study by Bessembinder [1], across 29,754 U.S. common stocks
from 1926 to 2025, nearly 60% destroyed shareholder wealth over their lifetimes
(median buy-and-hold return of -6.9%), and only 46 companies generated half of
the USD 91 trillion in net wealth creation—nearly double the concentration
observed in 2018 (89 companies for half of USD 43 trillion). A similar
concentration characterizes the Taiwan equity market: by October 2026,
cumulative investor accounts reached 14,827,469 (with 56.3% of new October
accounts opened by investors aged 30 or younger) [11], while TSMC alone
represents over 40% of total market capitalization [12]. In 2025, the Taiwan
benchmark surged +25.7% (+5,928.5 points, reaching TWD 94 trillion in market
value), driven disproportionately by AI supply-chain names (e.g., PCB and
memory suppliers posting +483% to +686% gains), whereas the OTC small-cap
index rose only 7.97% [14]. In 2026, approximately 90% of electronics stocks
outperforming the market had paid-in capital below TWD 8 billion, with
small/mid-cap electronics averaging 171% returns [13].

1.2 Core Challenges
Despite strong investor demand for systematic screening tools, four structural
challenges hinder ex-ante identification of long-horizon winners ("multibaggers"):

(1) Ex-Ante Winner Rarity and Active Management Failure: Ex-post traits of
    winning stocks do not trivially translate into ex-ante selection alpha. The
    S&P Global SPIVA Year-End 2025 Scorecard reports that 79% of active U.S.
    large-cap funds underperformed the S&P 500, and zero large-cap funds
    maintained top-quartile performance across the five-year 2021–2025 window
    [2].

(2) Survivorship and Look-Ahead Biases: Backtests omitting delisted firms
    systematically overstate annualized returns by 1–3 percentage points
    (30–100% cumulative inflation over 30 years) [15]. An open-source audit
    using `pitbacktest` demonstrated that 0 out of 41 major delisted/acquired
    U.S. stocks could be retrieved accurately from free Yahoo Finance feeds
    [15]. In the LLM era, look-ahead bias is compounded by pre-training data
    leakage [5, 6].

(3) Black-Box Overfitting: Complex machine learning models trained on
    unconstrained financial panels often fit regime-specific noise rather than
    durable fundamental drivers.

(4) LLM Numerical Hallucination and Trading Instability: While LLMs exhibit
    predictive value in textual news analysis [3], they are unreliable
    calculators. The FAITH benchmark [4] shows that even frontier models suffer
    10–20% error rates on multi-variable financial computations (and near-0%
    accuracy for smaller models). Furthermore, live trading benchmarks such as
    Alpha Arena [7] revealed that 31 of 38 autonomous AI trading accounts lost
    money due to overtrading and unstable decision-making.

1.3 Research Questions and Contributions
To address these gaps, we investigate three research questions:
- RQ1: Under strict point-in-time (PiT) constraints and delisted-stock
  inclusion, can interpretable tabular models outperform the 5% random base
  rate and simple single-factor rules in ranking five-year Taiwan
  multibaggers?
- RQ2: How sensitive is out-of-sample ranking quality to walk-forward macro
  regimes (2018–2020) and feature specifications?
- RQ3: Does a Program-of-Thoughts (PoT) separation with automated verification
  eliminate numerical hallucinations in generated investment research briefs?

The main contributions of this paper are threefold:
1. A Point-in-Time (PiT) Taiwan Equity Dataset Design: Utilizing FinMind
   historical filings, monthly revenues, and dividend-adjusted prices aligned to
   an annual April 30 decision cutoff, explicitly incorporating delisted firms.
2. Dual-Criterion Target Formulation and Model Comparison: Defining a strict
   composite label (top 5% five-year dividend-adjusted return AND revenue
   CAGR >= 10%) and benchmarking LightGBM, logistic regression, single-factor
   rules, and raw LLM baselines.
3. Verifiable PoT Agent Architecture: Decoupling deterministic financial
   computation from LLM narrative synthesis, enforced by a three-round
   programmatic numerical verifier.

1.4 Paper Organization
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related
literature on multibagger factor investing, machine learning stock selection,
and financial LLM agents, summarized in a structured comparison matrix.
Section 3 presents the proposed Multibagger Scout architecture, formal
mathematical definitions, algorithms, and an end-to-end execution walk-through.
Section 4 details the walk-forward experimental setup, hyperparameter tuning
protocol, evaluation metrics, and preliminary feasibility checks. Section 5
concludes the paper with key takeaways, limitations, and future work.

2. RELATED WORK
================================================================================

2.1 Domain Factor Investing and Multibagger Characteristics
Empirical asset pricing has long documented return skewness. Bessembinder [1]
established that long-term wealth creation is concentrated in a tiny fraction
of listed firms. Focusing specifically on extreme winners, Yartseva (2025)
[10] analyzed 464 U.S. ten-baggers from 2009 to 2024 (averaging 26x over 15
years, including 24 hundred-baggers) and identified seven recurring traits:
(1) small initial scale (median market cap ~USD 350M), (2) attractive
valuation (high book-to-market, P/B < 0.91 with positive earnings yielding
34.7% annual excess return vs. 12.8% for low B/M), (3) positive operating
margin, (4) high ROA coupled with disciplined asset growth (firms whose asset
growth outpaced EBITDA growth suffered a 22.8 percentage-point next-year
return penalty), (5) strong free cash flow (FCF) yield, (6) technical entry
near 12-month lows following pullbacks, and (7) stricter filtering during
rising-rate regimes. However, conditioning on ex-post ten-baggers does not
guarantee ex-ante precision when applied to an entire market universe.

2.2 Machine Learning for Equity Selection in Taiwan
Tree-based gradient boosting (e.g., LightGBM) and regularized linear models
have been widely applied to cross-sectional return prediction. Recent Taiwan
market studies (e.g., NSYSU 2025 comparing Elastic Net, LightGBM, and DNN on
2014–2024 TWSE general-industry stocks; NCCU multi-factor and ML momentum
studies) demonstrate that non-linear feature interactions improve short-to-
medium-term ranking, yet rarely evaluate five-year compound multibagger
horizons with full delisted-stock accounting.

2.3 Financial LLM Agents and Program-of-Thoughts Reasoning
Lopez-Lira and Tang (2026) [3] proved in the Journal of Financial Economics
that GPT-4 headline sentiment predicts next-day stock returns (especially for
small-cap and negative news), though alpha decays as adoption widens.
Multi-agent frameworks such as TradingAgents v5 [8] simulate analyst,
researcher, and trader personas; however, reproducibility audits reveal high
variance across temperature and random seeds [8], and live competitions like
Alpha Arena [7] show raw LLM trading agents frequently incur losses.

To curb arithmetic hallucinations documented in FAITH [4] and long-document
QA benchmarks (PHANTOM), Program of Thoughts (PoT) delegates computation to
external code execution. Recent Agentic RAG financial QA frameworks (2026)
[9] report that PoT reasoning eliminates 88% of arithmetic errors compared to
end-to-end LLM generation.

3. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY: MULTIBAGGER SCOUT
================================================================================

3.1 Problem Formulation and Mathematical Definitions
Let U_t denote the investable universe of Taiwan listed (TWSE) and OTC (TPEX)
equities at annual decision date t (set to April 30 of year y, following the
statutory deadline for Taiwan annual and Q1 financial disclosures), including
all firms that subsequently delist during (t, t + 5y].

(1) Dividend-Adjusted Wealth Return:
For stock i at decision date t, let P_{i,t}^{adj} be the dividend- and
split-adjusted closing price. The five-year cumulative return is defined as:

    R_{i,t}^{(5y)} = ( P_{i,t+5y}^{adj} / P_{i,t}^{adj} ) - 1            (1)

For firms delisted at tau in (t, t + 5y], P_{i,t+5y}^{adj} is replaced by the
final realizable liquidation/last-traded adjusted price at tau, preventing
survivorship truncation.

(2) Five-Year Revenue Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR):
Let Rev_{i,t} denote trailing twelve-month (TTM) revenue known at date t:

    CAGR_{i,t}^{Rev} = ( Rev_{i,t+5y} / Rev_{i,t} )^(1/5) - 1             (2)

(3) Point-in-Time (PiT) Constraint:
Every feature vector x_{i,t} is constructed strictly from the information set
F_t available on or before t:

    x_{i,t} = Phi( { D_{i,s} | s_pub(D_{i,s}) <= t } )                    (3)

where s_pub(D_{i,s}) denotes the official public filing or monthly revenue
announcement timestamp (10th of the following month in Taiwan).

(4) Composite Target Label:
Let Q_{0.95}(R_t^{(5y)}) be the 95th percentile of five-year returns across
universe U_t. Stock i is labeled as a multibagger (Y_{i,t} = 1) if and only
if:

    Y_{i,t} = I( R_{i,t}^{(5y)} >= Q_{0.95}(R_t^{(5y)})
                 AND CAGR_{i,t}^{Rev} >= 0.10 )                           (4)

requiring both extreme relative wealth creation and fundamental top-line
expansion.

3.2 System Architecture and Core Modules
Multibagger Scout adopts a layered architecture comprising seven modules:
1. User Interface (UI): Accepts screening queries (e.g., top-K candidates at
   date t, or single-stock deep dive).
2. Planner: Orchestrates tool invocations without performing arithmetic.
3. Data Tool: Queries FinMind APIs (financial statements, monthly revenue,
   adjusted prices, delisted registry) under PiT filtering.
4. Model Tool: Runs trained LightGBM / Logistic Regression models, computes
   TreeSHAP attributions, and retrieves k-nearest historical analogs (kNN).
5. Rules Engine: Enforces exclusion criteria (minimum liquidity, data
   completeness, listing tenure) and factor sanity checks.
6. Verifier: Programmatically cross-checks every numeric value in the draft
   report against Data Tool and Model Tool outputs.
7. Reporter: Formats the verified evidence into a structured brief containing
   bullish drivers, bearish risks, historical analogs, and disclaimers.

3.3 Feature Engineering and Exclusion Rules
Aligned with Yartseva [10] and Taiwan accounting standards, we construct 10 core
PiT features:
- Scale & Valuation: Log Market Cap, Price-to-Book (P/B), Earnings Yield.
- Profitability & Efficiency: Operating Margin, Return on Assets (ROA), Gross
  Margin stability.
- Growth & Capital Discipline: TTM Revenue Growth, Asset Growth minus EBITDA
  Growth (capturing over-investment risk [10]), Free Cash Flow (FCF) Yield.
- Price Structure: 12-month price drawdown / proximity to 12-month low.

Exclusion rules remove stocks with missing core financials at t, extreme illiquidity,
or fewer than 12 months of trading history prior to t.

3.4 Program-of-Thoughts (PoT) Separation and Algorithms
Following PoT principles [9], the LLM never computes ratios, returns, or ranks.
Algorithms 1 and 2 formalize dataset construction and agent execution.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm 1: Point-in-Time (PiT) Dataset Construction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input:  Decision years Y = {2013..2020}, FinMind raw tables (Prices, Fin, Rev)
Output: PiT panel dataset D = {(x_{i,t}, Y_{i,t})}
1:  for each year y in Y do
2:      t <- April 30 of year y
3:      U_t <- all TWSE/TPEX stocks active on t (including future delisted)
4:      for each stock i in U_t do
5:          Filter Fin, Rev, Prices where pub_date <= t
6:          if liquidity_ok(i, t) and completeness_ok(i, t) then
7:              x_{i,t} <- compute_10_features(Fin, Rev, Prices, t)
8:              R_{i,t} <- compute_5y_adj_return(Prices, i, t, t+5y)
9:              CAGR_{i,t} <- compute_5y_rev_cagr(Rev, i, t, t+5y)
10:         end if
11:     end for
12:     Compute Q_0.95 of R_{i,t} across valid U_t
13:     Assign Y_{i,t} <- 1 if (R_{i,t} >= Q_0.95 and CAGR_{i,t} >= 0.10) else 0
14:     Append valid (x_{i,t}, Y_{i,t}) to D
15: end for
16: return D
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm 2: Multibagger Scout Agent & Three-Round Verification
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input:  Query (stock_id or screen_date t), max_verify_rounds = 3
Output: Verified Research Brief B_verified
1:  Planner parses query and dispatches Data Tool(stock_id, t)
2:  Data Tool returns PiT metrics M_raw (FCF yield, P/B, ROA, Rev CAGR, etc.)
3:  Model Tool computes score s_i, TreeSHAP top drivers Phi_i, and kNN analogs A_i
4:  Rules Engine checks exclusions and flags risk warnings W_i
5:  Reporter (LLM) drafts structured brief B_draft using ONLY {M_raw, Phi_i, A_i, W_i}
6:  for round = 1 to max_verify_rounds do
7:      nums_extracted <- regex_parse_numbers(B_draft)
8:      mismatches <- compare_against_ground_truth(nums_extracted, {M_raw, Phi_i, A_i})
9:      if mismatches is empty then
10:         return B_draft  // Verified
11:     else
12:         B_draft <- Reporter_fix(B_draft, mismatches)
13:     end if
14: end for
15: return fallback_template({M_raw, Phi_i, A_i, W_i})  // Zero-hallucination safe mode
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.5 End-to-End Case Walk-Through
To illustrate operational execution, consider a single-stock query for a
Taiwan semiconductor candidate at decision date t = 2020-04-30:
Step 1 (Planner -> Data Tool): The Planner invokes `get_pit_financials` with
`cutoff="2020-04-30"`. The tool retrieves FY2019 audited statements (filed by
March 31, 2020) and March 2020 monthly revenue (announced by April 10, 2020),
returning deterministic values (e.g., ROA, P/B, FCF yield, TTM revenue growth).
Step 2 (Model Tool): LightGBM scores the candidate in the top decile,
TreeSHAP identifies FCF yield (+) and low asset-growth-minus-EBITDA-growth
(+) as primary positive contributors, while elevated P/B (-) acts as a drag.
The kNN module retrieves the 3 nearest historical profiles from 2013–2017.
Step 3 (Verifier & Reporter): The LLM synthesizes a balanced pro/con brief
citing exact tool outputs. The Verifier parses every percentage and ratio in
the draft; if any figure deviates from the tool JSON beyond rounding
tolerance (e.g., +/- 0.01%), it triggers a targeted correction round (up to 3
rounds) or falls back to a deterministic template.

4. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN AND EVALUATION PROTOCOL
================================================================================

4.1 Dataset and Walk-Forward Simulation Setup
The full sample spans 2013–2020 annual feature snapshots (decision date April
30 each year), with five-year forward return windows extending through 2025.
We employ a strict three-round expanding-window walk-forward protocol:
- Round 1: Train on 2013–2017 -> Test at 2018-04-30 (2018–2023 outcome).
- Round 2: Train on 2013–2018 -> Test at 2019-04-30 (2019–2024 outcome).
- Round 3: Train on 2013–2019 -> Test at 2020-04-30 (2020–2025 outcome).

These three test cohorts span distinct volatility regimes: the 2018 U.S.–China
trade war, the 2019 market rebound, and the 2020 COVID-19 V-shaped recovery.
We also note an important regime boundary: Taiwan's 2023–2026 AI supercycle
(and sudden structural shocks such as the November 6, 2026 single-day drop of
3,737 points triggered by AI data-center policy uncertainty [12]) underscores
that models trained on pre-AI cycles require ongoing out-of-sample monitoring.

4.2 Baselines and Hyperparameter Tuning Protocol
We benchmark Multibagger Scout against six baselines:
1. LightGBM (primary non-linear tabular model)
2. L2-regularized Logistic Regression (linear baseline)
3. Single-Factor Rules (Lowest P/B; Highest ROE)
4. Taiwan 50 ETF (0050) market benchmark
5. Random Sampling (expected 5% base rate for top-5% label)
6. Direct-LLM Zero-Shot Baseline (raw LLM prompting without PoT tools)

Hyperparameter Tuning: To prevent test-set leakage, zero hyperparameter
tuning is permitted on test folds (2018–2020). All hyperparameters are
selected exclusively via time-ordered cross-validation within each training
window:
- LightGBM: `max_depth` in {3, 4, 5}, `num_leaves` in {7, 15, 31},
  `learning_rate` in {0.01, 0.05}, `min_child_samples` in {20, 50},
  `reg_lambda` in {1.0, 5.0}, with early stopping on the last training year.
- Logistic Regression: L2 penalty `C` in {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0}.
- kNN Analog Retriever: `k` in {3, 5, 10} using standardized Euclidean distance.

4.3 Evaluation Metrics
Performance is evaluated across ranking quality, risk, and report fidelity:
- Precision@K (K = 10, 20, 50) and Recall@50: Fraction of top-K ranked stocks
  that achieve Y_{i,t} = 1, benchmarked against the <=5% random base rate.
- PR-AUC (Precision-Recall Area Under Curve): Appropriate for imbalanced ~5%
  positive class prevalence.
- Maximum Drawdown (MDD): Downside risk of equal-weighted top-K portfolios.
- Report Numerical Accuracy & Evidence Coverage: Percentage of numerical
  claims in generated briefs matching tool ground truth (target: ~100% via
  3-round Verifier, building on PoT's 88% error-reduction benchmark [9]).

4.4 Preliminary Feasibility and Data Availability Audit
At the current proposal stage, full walk-forward backtest metrics
(Precision@50, PR-AUC) are scheduled for execution rather than reported as
completed results. However, two foundational checks have been established:
1. Pipeline Feasibility: Preliminary data ingestion (`verify_data.py`) and
   point-in-time financial cross-checks against TSMC disclosures confirm API
   schema compatibility and alignment with April 30 cutoff rules.
2. Delisted-Stock Coverage Audit Plan: Motivated by `pitbacktest` showing 0/41
   delisted U.S. stocks available in yfinance [15], our first experimental
   milestone explicitly quantifies FinMind's historical coverage rate for
   delisted TWSE/TPEX tickers and documents any residual survivorship gaps.

5. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
================================================================================

5.1 Summary of Proposed System
We presented Multibagger Scout, a point-in-time AI research agent designed to
identify long-horizon high-growth equities in the Taiwan stock market. By
combining a delisted-inclusive PiT dataset, a dual-criterion five-year
multibagger label, interpretable tabular models (LightGBM/LR with TreeSHAP),
and a Program-of-Thoughts (PoT) verification loop, the system addresses both
financial backtest biases and LLM numerical hallucinations.

5.2 Key Strengths and Expected Contributions
Unlike black-box ML screeners or unconstrained LLM trading agents that suffer
from numerical errors (10–20%+ in FAITH [4]) and high failure rates (31/38
losing accounts in Alpha Arena [7]), Multibagger Scout enforces strict
separation of concerns: deterministic code computes all metrics and verifies
outputs, while the LLM provides structured, auditable pro/con reasoning.

5.3 Limitations and Future Work
Key limitations include potential gaps in free-tier delisted-stock histories
on FinMind, regime shift risks between the 2013–2020 training/test windows and
the 2023–2026 AI supercycle, and the absence of transaction-cost/slippage
modeling in pure ranking metrics. Future work will: (1) complete the three-round
walk-forward backtest and report Precision@50, PR-AUC, and statistical
significance tests against the 5% base rate; (2) audit FinMind delisted
coverage; and (3) incorporate Taiwan-specific institutional chip-flow and
news-sentiment features under strict PiT timestamps.

quantitative information synthesis and does not constitute financial or
investment advice.
